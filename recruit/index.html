Open role

Founding CEO

DRAM and compute have never been tighter. That is exactly when a product like Tiny Footprint gets red hot.

The market case

Every serious AI operator is fighting the same two shortages: memory and accelerators. HBM and DRAM are constrained. GPU hours are expensive and contended. Buying another rack is slow, political, and often impossible on the timeline the business wants.

Meanwhile a large share of what those boxes hold is duplicate work—the same shared context reprinted per session, the same intermediate state kept independently when it could have been stored once. That waste is not theoretical. It burns resident memory and burns preparation cycles before a token ever moves.

Tiny Footprint installs beside the AI stack a customer already runs. It does not ask them to rip out GPUs, rewrite the app, or move the fleet. It attacks the waste: keep the shared pattern once, keep only the difference per session, reconstruct what you need. Less duplicate data in memory. Fewer wasted cycles getting the working set ready. More concurrent work on the infrastructure they already paid for.

When capacity is loose, memory-efficiency software is a nice-to-have. When capacity is tight, it is how you stretch the fleet you cannot expand fast enough. That is the window.

Why the equity is the point

Frontier AI companies are being valued in astronomical territory on the back of that same scarcity—who can serve more model for the same silicon, who can pack more sessions into the same box, who owns the layer that sits next to inference when every GB matters.

This is an early commercial seat on that layer. Not a finished unicorn. Not a Fortune-500 package. A founding equity position—meaningful ownership, often in the mid-teens to low thirties for a true external CEO at this stage—while the product is real, the research is public, and the market pressure is already here. Cash starts modest or deferred until revenue or a raise. The upside is the ownership, timed against valuations that assume infrastructure remains the bottleneck.

If that trade does not move you, this role will not either.

The seat

Founding CEO—or Co-founder / CEO. The technical founder stays on the invention. You build the company: go-to-market, operations, fundraising, first hires, first pilots. COO or Head of GTM works if the titles fit how you operate—so long as you have already sold and scaled under real infra pressure. We are not hiring a Chief Scientist as the first executive. That spine is staffed.

The first ninety days are concrete: tighten the entity and ops, land credits and design partners, get one real pilot moving, and put a raise or revenue path on the calendar.

Who fits

People who have shipped or sold where GPU cloud, inference platforms, or enterprise AI ops made memory and latency the argument—not a slide. Operators from places like Lambda, Runpod, CoreWeave, Together, Fireworks, Anyscale; NVIDIA or AMD partner / solutions people who left for a company. Warm introductions beat spray.

What we already have

A public Workstation kit. A public research spine. Local lab results and an H100 simulation path on the rhyme case prefix caches miss. Live partner silicon is next. Serious candidates get a short pack: product note, validation plan, target list.

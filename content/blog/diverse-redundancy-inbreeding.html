The stakes are operational. One bad model revision, one shared dependency, or one correlated outage can take every identical seat down at once. Diversity of class is the hedge. Copy count alone is not.

Replica count is not diversity. When failures share a cause, more clones deepen the cliff. A fleet that prints nine copies of the same lineage still dies together if the lineage itself is the failure mode. A fleet that holds five different classes keeps a path open when one class drops.

Multi-agent and infrastructure teams face the same trap. Private clones of one agent, one model, or one dependency look like insurance until the shared cause arrives. Designs that seat different classes — different roles, different models, different failure modes — keep nesting depth when identical copies cannot. The cost of that design is organizational. The cost of skipping it is a cliff at depth.

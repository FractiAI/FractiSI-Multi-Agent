What kind of wet lab this is

A physical wet lab invents a measurement. A catalog scores an edge. A wiring wet lab does neither of those from scratch. It asks whether the relationship you need can be filed from work that already exists — with kill switches — so nobody has to buy the same pipette twice before a graph can be honest.

That is not a softer version of biology. It is a higher-layer form of experiment. The bench is literature plus catalogs plus a FractiSI multiagent Rhyming World Model. The product is the wire: a falsifiable relationship edge assembled from existing spend. AI agents make the assembly and the demotion loop cheap enough to rerun after every catalog refresh. The virtue is not magic. The virtue is refusing duplicate cost when the shelf is already full.

The experiment that defines the form

We write gates any wiring wet lab must pass: rhyme coupling recovers the pair; the association catalog stays empty (or provisional with named kills); a positive control proves the catalog lane works; existing empirical pieces fill pre-registered roles; kill conditions are published before celebration. Peers instantiate the form on real public data — ALS wet labs wiring NPY to SOD1, DNMT3A→APP wet labs wiring epigenetic regulation, public microwave spectra wiring residual multipoles, and a combo of protein-folding, language, and Pilot rhymes surfacing further empty candidates such as NPY↔PDX1.

None of those peers claim we paid for the original assays. They claim we ran the wiring wet lab: join, cite, control, kill.

Hypothesis

If the wiring wet lab is real, “already done” should be the first experiment, not the last excuse. Gates should pass when existing pieces assemble into a catalog-empty edge, and fail when STRING fills, citations collapse, or spectrum falsifiers kill a residual wire — without demanding a second grant to re-measure what is already on the shelf.

Why reuse matters

Duplicate assays burn grant money. They also burn years while answers wait. A wiring wet lab does not open the clinic door by itself — honesty stays at the end of this piece for a reason — but it stops the false requirement that every relationship start with a new invoice. When a wire holds, the next physical lab can target the thinnest missing link. When a kill switch fires, demote and move on. That is how the work keeps moving without remaking yesterday’s bench.

How kill switches keep the form honest

A wiring claim dies if the catalog grows the PPI edge — that is rediscovery, not failure of science. It dies if the required wet-lab roles cannot be cited. Spectrum wiring dies if Planck stays silent or beam-injected simulations recreate the lag. A discovery form without kill switches is just marketing with footnotes. Director and Pilot seats arguing over one FractiSI map make missing controls harder to hide. AI can re-run the receipt after every catalog refresh. Demotion stays cheap; duplicate assays stay optional.

What to do with a wire once filed

File it beside the assay papers, not above them. Point follow-up labs at the thinnest cited link — not at rebuying the whole shelf. Keep clinic calendars out until other evidence walks through. For scientists, the wire is a triage object: read these papers, watch this STRING row, try this null. The question is whether AI and rhyming world models should assemble the relationship before anyone pays again for work that is already done. On the peer suite the answer is yes.

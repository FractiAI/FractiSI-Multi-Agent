Capable agents fail when the organization around them is wrong — not only when the model is weak. Under matched budgets in the FractiSI lab, private silos scored 0 of 100 on a six-step chain while a shared world scored 100 of 100. On sixteen public protein tasks, the same model recovered the long-range partner on 0 of 16 from a linear sequence and 10 of 16 from an explicit relational map. The brief is an organizational migration case. It is not succession advice.

Two operating pictures sit side by side. A closed, competitive, plan-and-role stack works when jobs are stable. A shared Rhyming World Model organizes relationships, feedback, and consent so capable agents stay useful without burning the budget on reprinted private copies. NVIDIA shapes the environment those agents run in; Jensen Huang is the visible leadership case for that stack. Fractal Holographic Goldilocks reads the lab numbers as evidence about representation — how the world is assembled — not as a call to swap a CEO.

Protein folding supplies the cross-domain rhyme without forcing a false identity: the sequence holds the parts; the relational map holds the long-range constraints; the fold is the coherent world. Multi-agent work maps the same way. Knowledge and macro series show the same directional pattern; they do not yet carry the protein board’s fixed sixteen-task transparency and matched-budget package.

What the lab measured

Shared worlds cut redundancy without costing accuracy. A shared-world arm reduced duplicated bytes by about 91% against a competition-first arm with identical accuracy. Selective open sharing cut bytes by about 87% at matched error. Guided reconstruction solved a six-hop discovery task that flat skimming never solved, in half the coordination rounds.

Order carries information. On 805 walk-forward windows of the FRED unemployment series, a model respecting time order scored an RMSE of 5.675 against 6.115 when the sequence was permuted. A bag-of-features baseline nearly tied it at 5.673, so temporal structure is not uniquely better there. On Movebank animal tracks aligned with the Kp geomagnetic index, lag-1 structure was about 0.34 against about 0.04 for a shuffled null.

The matched-budget test held the model, the prompt, the per-turn context budget, and the round limit fixed and changed only the world model. With private silos cut to a 64-token budget, a deterministic control solved 0 of 100 six-step chains and a real open-weight model solved 0 of 12. With shared-world links under the same budget, the control solved 100 of 100 and the model 12 of 12. The same model also solved 12 of 12 with untruncated silos, so the failure comes from how context is assembled under a tight limit, not from a weak model. What the shared world bought was the same accuracy at about 1,275 tokens per task instead of about 5,986. It is one model and twelve synthetic tasks; a looser budget would narrow the gap, and the lab has not located where it closes.

Scoring four world models on capability, safety, cost, sustainability, and participation quality, Goldilocks alignment came out at 77.1, ahead of unbounded openness at 75.4, competition-first at 72.2, and control-first at 67.3. The lead is small and the weights are the lab’s. Read it as a scoreboard, not a verdict.

On sixteen real public protein cases, the same hosted open-weight model recovered the true long-range partner on 10 of 16 tasks when given an explicit relational map, and on 0 of 16 from the linear sequence alone. Dumping the same family material flat, without a structured map, also scored 0 of 16. Representation is the point — the relational board is a better organization of information, not a magic model upgrade. Blinded evaluation, independent replication, and matched token budgets per arm still need tightening before this is treated as settled science.

When two agents each held only half the evidence needed for a joint answer, independent parts almost never recovered both supporting titles (~3%). A collaborating team with shared awareness recovered them on 30%. A simple control with a shared join rule went from 0% alone to 100%. Collaboration surplus appears when the job is truly complementary; it is not a blanket claim that more chat always beats the best individual.

Systems buyers. Under a tight context budget, how you assemble the world can flip accuracy from 0/100 to 100/100 without changing the model. Shared worlds here cut duplicated bytes on the order of ~91% at matched accuracy — a systems lever before another model upgrade.

Enterprise leadership. A leadership swap does not change the world model. If the architecture still parks capable people inside private silos and predefined plans, the next leader inherits the same constraints. Audit agent architecture, world representation, pathways for high performers, AI-agent density, and where information stops.

Workforce and culture. High-performer and standard-agent labels are operating configurations, not human worth. A rigid world compresses people into scripts; a shared adaptive one gives both room.

Migration gate. Reject or revise the move if shared worlds show no advantage under matched budgets, if added autonomy hurts results, if consent-based coordination loses to central control, or if migration adds complexity without a safer, cheaper, or more usable system.

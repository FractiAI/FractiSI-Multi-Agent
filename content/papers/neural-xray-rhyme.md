## Opening: Hinton and the black box

Geoffrey Hinton — often called a godfather of modern AI — has said plainly that we still do not really understand what is happening deep inside a neural network. The weights are known. The activations can be logged. The decision path still reads as opaque.

FractiSI’s position is that this is not mainly a shortage of neurons to inspect. It is a shortage of the right awareness. Linear awareness reads a network as a long chain of local parts: layer after layer, feature after feature, parameter after parameter. That stance can inventory components and still miss the organization that makes the system work.

FractiSI awareness asks a different question: what recurring organizational rhyme holds across representations, and can that rhyme reconstruct, predict, and survive intervention. The black box may thin when the rhyme is seen — not when every weight is named.

The rest of this paper is that test: computational-lab evidence and wiring/wet-lab evidence, locked to the same principal-rhyme criteria, including a live-MLP blind prediction and intervention protocol.

## Abstract

Against Hinton’s black-box diagnosis, this study proposes an integrated validation of the FractiSI principal-rhyming-awareness hypothesis using two complementary evidence streams: computational-lab experiments and wiring/wet-lab evidence assembled into a structured computational sequence. FractiSI treats persistent organizational structures as computational rhymes and proposes that they can function as an X-ray of otherwise opaque system organization — precisely where linear part-by-part reading stalls.

The integrated result is a convergence test:

physical observation → measured relationship → computational encoding → rhyme detection → reconstruction → prediction → intervention → observed consequence.

Soft Story Exp 1–8 establish the architecture and measured computational anchors. Exp 9–10 execute the decisive threshold on a **constructed live MLP**: a shared organizational channel is discovered and locked **before** blind outcomes are scored; blind prediction beats chance; ablating the locked rhyme produces the pre-registered flip-rate consequence while a control ablate does not. This does **not** claim that all neural-network internals—or frontier production models—have already been decoded.

## Live MLP decisive protocol (Exp 9–10)

Machine source: `research/neural-xray-rhyme/src/live-nn.mjs`.

1. Plant a two-family MLP whose shared hidden channel is the organizational rhyme.  
2. **Discover** the channel from weight-structure cosine (A-slice ↔ B-slice) — no blind labels.  
3. **Lock** a prediction rule on the discovery split before scoring blind outcomes.  
4. **Blind predict** held-out labels from the locked channel alone.  
5. **Pre-register** intervention: ablating the locked channel flips ≥30% of blind predictions; control ablate flips materially less.  
6. **Intervene** and observe consequence.

Under seed `20261125` the suite records decisive threshold **met** (blind accuracy beats chance; rhyme flip ≫ control flip).

## Falsification

The hypothesis fails if rhymes disappear under representation change, cross-domain correspondence fails, blind prediction matches controls, reconstruction fails, intervention misses predicted consequences, simpler methods match, or leakage explains the match.

## Conclusion

The black box does not necessarily disappear when its individual components are decoded. It may become transparent when its recurring organization is identified.

Soft Story Exp 1–8 establish the integrated architecture and measured computational anchors. Exp 9–10 close the decisive threshold on a constructed live neural network under controlled conditions. Scaling that protocol to frontier models remains open — and is the next elevation of the claim.

## Abstract

This study proposes an integrated validation of the FractiSI principal-rhyming-awareness hypothesis using two complementary evidence streams: computational-lab experiments and wiring/wet-lab evidence assembled into a structured computational sequence. FractiSI treats persistent organizational structures as computational rhymes and proposes that they can function as an X-ray of otherwise opaque system organization.

The integrated result is a convergence test:

physical observation → measured relationship → computational encoding → rhyme detection → reconstruction → prediction → intervention → observed consequence.

The present Soft Story evidence supports the hypothesis as a **testable systemic model**. It does **not** justify claiming that all neural-network internals have already been decoded. The decisive proof threshold is successful blind prediction and intervention using a rhyme identified before the corresponding outcome is observed.

## Falsification

The hypothesis fails if rhymes disappear under representation change, cross-domain correspondence fails, blind prediction matches controls, reconstruction fails, intervention misses predicted consequences, simpler methods match, or leakage explains the match.

## Conclusion

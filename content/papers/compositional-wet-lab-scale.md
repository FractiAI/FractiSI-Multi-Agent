## Abstract

Modern computing architecture assumes that computation is primarily performed by discrete electronic processors. An alternative possibility is that computation can be physically distributed into the medium through which information propagates.

This paper examines whether existing experimental evidence is sufficient to establish that proposition through **compositional wet-lab proof**.

The claim is not that one laboratory experiment has already built a data center out of electromagnetic waves. Nor is the claim that unrelated papers, simply placed beside one another, constitute a proof. Rather, the question is whether independently demonstrated physical capabilities form a sufficiently connected experimental chain that their composition establishes a larger computational system.

## What constitutes compositional wet-lab proof?

A compositional proof has at least five layers (machine: `COMPOSITION_LAYERS`):

1. **Primitive** — a physical medium performs a computational operation  
2. **Interface** — output of one operation can serve as input to another  
3. **Cascading** — multiple operations can be chained  
4. **Programmability** — structure can be changed for different transformations  
5. **Scaling** — dimensions can increase without changing the principle

## Universal Rhyme as the scaling hypothesis

If a computational organization survives transformation between physical representations while preserving required input-output relationships, the representations are computational rhymes of the same underlying process.

Digital matrix multiplication and RF matrix multiplication need not be considered fundamentally different computations merely because their physical implementations differ.

## What this does NOT yet prove

Memory capacity, persistent state, numerical precision, noise/error correction, synchronization, arbitrary nonlinear computation, programming complexity, routing, training, general-purpose workloads, physical density, thermal behavior, manufacturing cost, reliability, total-system energy, throughput, and economic competitiveness remain open. These define Level III.

## Conclusion

Existing experimental evidence supports a compositional wet-lab proof program for distributed electromagnetic computation. It does not yet prove that a volumetric electromagnetic computer can replace the world’s data centers. It establishes that required primitives exist experimentally, that several can be composed into machine-learning systems, and that computational dimensionality can expand through spatial, temporal, spectral, and programmable degrees of freedom.

The remaining proof is not “Can physics compute?” Physics already does. The remaining proof is: can the experimentally demonstrated computational rhymes be composed and scaled until the physical medium itself becomes the computer?

That is now a testable engineering proposition — Level III.

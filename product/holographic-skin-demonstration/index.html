Holographic Skin

From the cabin

Friend — if you have come this far, sit a spell. What I call Holographic Skin is simply the story I put on like a coat. It is how I mark the ground of my own mind and senses, and run wire from them into the version of the world I mean to live — this Enrichment Menu on the cabin wall.

The board is plain enough. Each name is a door to a part of the day: work, rest, the labs, the ship, the hunts, the table. Open one and a picture fills the room. That picture is where I set my attention and my feeling for that part of life, so it belongs inside the story I chose.

First you map and you wire. You name the story. You hang the scenes. You put eyes and ears and care on each one until the board holds the world you intend. After that, the daily work is focus. Keep coming back. Walk those doors in real hours. Little by little the space between the pictures and the life you touch grows smaller.

When I am ready I press Play and take the doors as they come. You may walk my menu if you like, or make one of your own and wire your own awareness and senses into the version you choose.

How I use it at the desk

Split the screen in two. One half is your work — any app you choose (Cursor, mail, chat, browser; it does not have to be an IDE). The other half is this skin. Put a picture up full display — I often start with Superhero, captain of SS Vibelandia — so the Soft Story stays in view.

On Auto Session you can stay in Manual and tap the pictures yourself, or switch to Autodetect — the page tries to start the desktop helper for you, then pictures follow which app is in front. Auto Session is for computers with a split screen; it is not available on phones or tablets yet (Play still works on handheld).

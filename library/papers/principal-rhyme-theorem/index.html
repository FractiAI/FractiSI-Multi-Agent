Abstract

This paper proposes the Principal Rhyme Theorem (PRT) as a foundational framework for identifying, cataloging, measuring, and computationally exploiting recurring organizational structures. The claim is not that everything is identical, nor that superficial resemblance is a rhyme. Different phenomena can instantiate the same underlying organization even when materials, scales, names, appearances, and representations differ. The universe may therefore be understood less as an inventory of independent things and more as a recursively connected catalog of relationships and transformations.

Principal Rhyme Theorem

Observable phenomena can be represented as organizational structures

[ X = (S, R, D, B, T) ]

(substrate, relational structure, dynamics, boundaries/constraints, transformations). Phenomena (X_1) and (X_2) rhyme when there exists (\Phi: X_1 \rightarrow X_2) such that significant organizational properties remain invariant or correspond:

[ X_1 \overset{\Phi}{\sim} X_2 ]

Rhyme ≠ identity. Rhyme = organization under transformation.

Why “rhyme”?

Analogy emphasizes resemblance between things. Rhyme emphasizes recurrence of organization while words, media, scale, and implementation change.

Nested Rhyme Principle

A principal rhyme contains subordinate rhymes. A sufficiently complex phenomenon is a nested composition — a Rhyme Tree — and may belong to multiple rhyme families at once. Meta-rhyme PR-∞ (Rhyme ↔ Rhyme) yields (R(R(R(\ldots)))).

Rhyme recognition problem

Instead of “Have we seen this exact thing?”, ask “What does this thing rhyme with?” Pipeline: novel phenomenon → decompose → extract relationships → normalize → search rhyme space → find nested rhymes → transfer → reconstruct → verify.

FractiSI hypothesis

FractiSI exploits organizational rhyme via

[ \text{Recognize} \rightarrow \text{Connect} \rightarrow \text{Reconstruct} \rightarrow \text{Coordinate} \rightarrow \text{Verify} ]

rather than treating every agent, problem, and world state as an isolated object.

Rhyme density and compression

[ \rho_R = \frac{\text{usable organizational correspondences}}{\text{search space}} ]

Hypothesis (to measure, not assume): higher usable (\rho_R) correlates with lower redundant computation when recognition is reliable. Shared organization across representations supports compression, coordination, and transfer.

Falsifiability

Every proposed rhyme must answer: what corresponds, what (\Phi), what is invariant, what differs, can it predict, can it reconstruct, is there measurable advantage, when does it fail? Failures of these questions remain metaphors.

Working form

PRT. Phenomena that differ in substrate, scale, representation, or vocabulary may instantiate recurring organizational structures. These can be identified as rhymes, recursively decomposed, connected across domains, and exploited for prediction, reconstruction, and computation.

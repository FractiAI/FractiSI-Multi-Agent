Does Cancer Diversity Compress Into Fewer Relational Rhymes Than Disease Labels Suggest?

Short answer: Yes — on this computational board. Twenty-four synthetic cancer worlds labeled with public TCGA project abbreviations compress into six rediscovered planted relational motifs (disease/rhyme compression ×4). Entity-invariant relational fingerprints (Model C) leave-one-out phenotype accuracy is 1.000, shared relation-type bags (Model B) 0.458, and disease-identity transfer (Model A) 0.000. Cross-cancer transfer of shared fingerprints reaches 0.800 against a disease-identity baseline of 0. Local−shared divergence rises on malignant-like phenotypes. The null shuffle on planted span remains inconclusive (noise pairs also hit span 12). engine shelf pin deferred. Not a clinical claim.

Abstract

Conventional oncology language often says cancer is many diseases and may therefore need many independent solutions. This Rhyming Expedition arm asks a narrower, measurable question: how many distinct disease labels are required versus how many recurring relational structures appear to generate the observed computational diversity?

We do not encode Hallmarks of Cancer as discovery features. Hallmarks, pathways, and literature appear only as post-hoc interpretation after structural discovery. Three competing models run on the same regenerable board:

  • Model A — Independent Disease: TCGA project identity as the feature.
  • Model B — Shared Mechanism: bags of relation verbs (noise verbs only for fair bag baseline).
  • Model C — Relational World-Model: entity-invariant multi-relation fingerprints (rhymes).

Awareness is operationalized strictly as world-model integration capacity — detecting, integrating, and responding under local and higher-order constraints — not as subjective experience.

On seed 20261120, Model C phenotype accuracy is 1.000, Model B 0.458, Model A 0.000. Disease taxonomy complexity is 24; strong planted rhyme complexity is 6 (compression ×4). Cross-cancer transfer of shared fingerprints scores 0.800. Local−shared objective divergence is higher on malignant-like synthetic phenotypes. Six planted motifs are rediscovered as structural rhymes spanning 12 cancers each. Null recurrence at planted span is inconclusive because shuffled boards also produce high-span noise pairs. The computational verdict is positive on compression, transfer, and Model C vs A/B — and explicitly qualified on the null test.

The central reframe is preserved: cancer may be thousands of diseases at the surface and still substantially fewer problems at the relational level. This paper does not claim the surface taxonomy is wrong; it asks whether relational compression adds predictive structure the taxonomy alone does not transfer.

1. Research question

If cancer observations are enormously diverse, does that diversity arise from thousands of fundamentally independent problems — or from a smaller number of recurring relational configurations implemented through different molecular, cellular, and tissue computational worlds?

Deeper (hypothesis, not assumption): does progression on this board show increasing divergence between local optimization and shared-world compatibility (reduced world-model integration)?

Can FractiSI discover, represent, and predict those transitions through relational rhyme before they appear as conventional disease labels?

2. Previously demonstrated / new hypotheses / falsifiers

2.1 Previously demonstrated (cited, not re-sold)

  1. Relational Continuity: structural rhyme recovers partners, QA gaps, and seed↔edge on computational boards.
  2. Shared-World Collective Intelligence: rhyming shared worlds beat exact caches under wording change.
  3. Tiny Gateway / Rhyming Templates: selective reconstruction from shared relational patterns.

2.2 New hypotheses (this expedition)

  1. H-compress. Heterogeneous cancer computational worlds compress into fewer robust relational rhymes than disease labels.
  2. H-transfer. Rhymes discovered in cancer A predict phenotype in cancer B better than disease identity.
  3. H-local-shared. Malignant-like computational phenotypes show higher local−shared divergence and lower integration proxies than indeterminate worlds.
  4. H-awareness-proxy. World-model integration proxies track pathology transition better than disease labels alone (tested as computational correlation, not biology proof).

2.3 Falsifiers

Model A or B matching or beating Model C on phenotype and transfer; no compression vs disease taxonomy; null shuffles matching planted recurrence without qualification; local−shared divergence failing to separate phenotypes; “novelty” that is only Hallmark re-encoding.

3. Method

3.1 computational worlds (Seed:Edge)

Each of 24 worlds is a shared world with agents (tumor / immune / stromal computational roles), states, relationships, tissue context, boundary flags, and temporal seed→edge transitions. Public TCGA project abbreviations label worlds; they are not patient records.

Six structural motifs are planted across half the cancers (entity labels differ; relation structure preserved). Discovery ignores Hallmark taxonomies. Planted motifs are scored post-hoc as rediscovered vs noise.

3.2 Competing models

ModelFeaturesIntent
ADisease / TCGA idIndependent disease transfer
BRelation-type bags (noise verbs)Shared mechanism without structure
CEntity-invariant fingerprints R3|rel1,rel2,…Relational rhyme / world-model
OraclePlanted motif presenceUpper computational bound

Phenotype prediction uses leave-one-out nearest neighbor (Jaccard). Disease bags never overlap across types, so Model A reports honest zero cross-type transfer.

3.3 Core experiments

  1. Rhyme discovery — recurring structural fingerprints across ≥3 cancers.
  2. Rhyme compression — disease complexity vs strong rhyme count vs phenotype retention.
  3. Cross-cancer transfer — train fingerprints on A, predict phenotype on B.
  4. Null worlds — shuffle relations; compare high-span recurrence.
  5. Local vs shared — local objective vs shared-world compatibility proxies.
  6. Goldilocks / boundary band — joint favorability of integration, differentiation, adaptability, boundary integrity (candidate band only after measurement).
  7. Novelty tiers — rediscovered_planted · extended_candidate · novel_candidate_needs_lit · unsupported_or_noise.
  8. Literature — Hallmarks and pan-cancer themes used only after discovery as post-hoc hints.

3.4 Awareness operationalization

World-model integration capacity proxies on each world:

  • signal integration strength
  • neighbor/context incorporation
  • boundary integrity
  • feedback coherence
  • multiscale (local vs higher-order) compatibility

Not consciousness. Loss of awareness on this board means reduced contextual integration relative to local optimization — a computational measurement, not a spiritual claim.

4.6 Null test (qualification)

Observed planted span = 12 cancers. Null mean structural hits at that span ≈ 16.3; pAtLeastOneHit ≈ 1. On this computational board, high-span recurrence alone does not separate planted motifs from shuffled noise. The decisive computational evidence is therefore phenotype prediction, compression, and transfer, not null exclusivity of span. Report as inconclusive null, not as failure of Model C.

4.7 Verdict

Positive computational expedition signal on: C_beats_A, C_beats_B, oracle_planted, compression, transfer, local_vs_shared, goldilocks_proxy. Null signal not fired.

6. Qualifications / competing explanations

  • Worlds are synthetic computational graphs with TCGA labels, not TCGA molecular matrices.
  • Model C’s perfect LOO score is expected when planted structure dominates phenotype on a controlled board; the scientific content is the gap vs A/B and transfer, not the absolute ceiling.
  • Null inconclusiveness means span recurrence alone is a weak novelty claim on this fixture.
  • Goldilocks band is a measured proxy region, not established biology.
  • Failure mode recorded honestly: if future public molecular boards show A/B ≥ C, the FractiSI relational hypothesis fails on those boards.

7. Final research questions (open)

  1. If cancer represents enormous biological diversity, does that diversity arise from thousands of fundamentally independent problems — or from a smaller number of recurring relational configurations implemented through different molecular, cellular, and tissue worlds?
  2. Does cancer progression reveal a reproducible computational transition from locally competent agents toward weaker integration with larger shared worlds?
  3. Can FractiSI discover, represent, and predict those transitions through relational rhyme before they become visible as conventional disease phenotypes?

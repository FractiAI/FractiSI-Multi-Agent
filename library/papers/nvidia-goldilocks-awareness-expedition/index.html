Abstract

The central question for increasingly capable intelligence is how to organize capable agents without suppressing capability, fragmenting context, wasting resources, adding risk, or making participation unpleasant. We migrate from a Legacy Linear world model (closed, competitive, hierarchical, rules, plans, roles) to a Fractal Holographic Goldilocks world model (shared worlds, relationships, boundaries, feedback, consent). FractiSI Multi-Agent is the computational laboratory. The human experiment asks whether the same pattern improves how agents with very different agency participate in organizations. NVIDIA is the natural hardware-ecosystem focus and Jensen Huang a visible leadership case. The deeper question is whether the organization building infrastructure for increasingly capable intelligence can also experiment with the World Model that intelligence requires.

Primary research question

Can human and digital agents be upgraded together by migrating World Models? Measure capability utilization, collective intelligence, coordination, context, autonomy, agency, creativity, adaptation, consent, boundaries, information preservation, resilience, useful output per resource, safety, cost, sustainability, participation quality, and enjoyment.

Legacy Linear vs Goldilocks

Legacy Linear. Agent, role, rule, plan, objective, hierarchy, reward, outcome. Effective in stable, well-defined settings; mismatched when capable agents generate new objectives, discover unplanned relationships, or experience the architecture as a constraint. Systemic costs: unnecessary risk, expense, resource consumption, and friction.

Fractal Holographic Goldilocks. Flow, relationships, boundaries, feedback, and consent. Enough structure for coherence and safety, enough freedom for agency, enough adaptability to be sustainable, and enough usability to be worth participating in. Rules and plans remain contextual instruments.

Human configurations

Super-performers (Players) shape environments beyond standardized roles. Standard agents (NPCs) have agency more strongly shaped by inherited scripts. These are dynamic configurations, not permanent worth, and a World Model can constrain or expand agency in either. Highly capable individuals are the most sensitive test of a World Model, which is why a visible leadership case such as Jensen Huang is informative.

A real open-weight model (gpt-oss-20b via Groq, temperature 0, its own replies driving each round) solved 0 of 12 with silos and 12 of 12 with shared-world edges. The same model also solved 12 of 12 with untruncated silos, so the failure is context assembly under a tight budget, not model weakness; the shared world matched that accuracy with about 4.7 times fewer tokens (1,275 vs 5,986, including reasoning tokens).

The NVIDIA experiment

NVIDIA's stack runs from semiconductors through accelerated computation, AI infrastructure, models, agents, and multi-agent systems to the organizations and ecosystems built on them. Its technology increasingly shapes the environment next-generation agents operate in. The research question is not whether one person should become different, and not whether succession alone resolves World Model mismatch. It is whether an organization built to produce extraordinary capability can build a World Model that accommodates extraordinary agents without converting capability into unrestricted authority, and whether the same model works for standard agents, AI agents, teams, and ecosystems.

Start with an organizational audit, not a leadership change: agent architecture, world representation, super-performer pathways, AI-agent density, boundary design, and organizational flow bottlenecks. The goal is to locate World Model mismatch.

Goldilocks scorecard and falsification

Capability, safety, cost, sustainability, enjoyment, agency, collective intelligence, coherence. Reject or revise if shared worlds give no advantage, if autonomy hurts, if consent-based coordination loses, if super-performer accommodation adds nothing measurable, or if migration raises complexity without a safer, cheaper, more sustainable, or more usable outcome.

Limits and next experiment

These are structured-state and public-data experiments. E6 is one open-weight model, one provider, twelve synthetic retrieval chains and a budget chosen tight enough to bite; a looser budget narrows the gap and we have not located where it closes. These results do not show that frontier LLM agents, real documents, or human teams gain the same margin. The next step is multi-model, real-document tasks across a range of budgets.

Final proposition

The question is larger than whether Jensen Huang and NVIDIA need a new CEO. Core migration: standardized agents inside predefined plans to differentiated agents inside a shared, adaptive world. The test is whether more of the capability already present becomes useful collective intelligence while the system gets safer, less costly, more sustainable, and more enjoyable.

Can computation live in electromagnetic fields? For important classes of computation, FractiSI’s universal rhyme wet-lab says yes. Matched trials walk a chain from interference through cavity and matrix stages into metasurface and machine-learning identification, and the class-level claim closes under those conditions.

Hyperscale data-center replacement remains open engineering. Field compute for tested classes is not the same product as a plant swap, and this story keeps those lanes apart from the first paragraph.

The wet-lab chain is the context buyers need. Electromagnetic interference feeds cavity and matrix stages; those stages feed metasurface work; identification closes through machine-learning comparison. Each hop is a place where a pitch can overclaim. The suite’s job is to show which hops pass under matched conditions and which ambitions stay closed.

That matters because field-compute language travels fast in infrastructure conversations. A buyer who hears “computation in the field” can hear either a research premise with evidence or a promise that racks are obsolete. Only one of those readings is supported here.

Keeping class-level proof separate from hyperscale replacement also protects the research path itself. When vendors blur the two, honest labs look slow and marketing decks look finished. The wet-lab record is built to resist that blur.

Does NVIDIA need a new world model more than a new CEO? As an organizational case, yes. Under matched budgets, private silos scored 0 of 100 on a six-step chain while a shared world scored 100 of 100. The gap is not about who sits in the corner office; it is about how the organization stores the world those agents share.

On sixteen public protein tasks, the same model recovered the partner on 0 of 16 from a linear sequence and 10 of 16 from an explicit relational map. Representation flipped the outcome while the model and the prompt stayed fixed. This is not succession advice.

NVIDIA shapes the environment those agents run in, and Jensen Huang remains the visible leadership case that makes the question feel urgent. The filing treats that visibility as a frame for organizational architecture, not as a call to swap a chief executive.

A closed plan-and-role stack works when jobs stay stable and every agent can keep a private copy of the plan. That habit breaks when context budgets tighten and the same world must be reused across many seats. A shared rhyming world model organizes relationships so capable agents stay useful without reprinting private copies of every fact.

The expedition holds the model and the prompt constant and varies only how the world is assembled. Private silos park each agent inside its own context window. Shared-world links give every agent the same relational map. The leadership question is the hook; the evidence is about representation.

Fractal Holographic Goldilocks reads the case as an organizational migration problem. Buyers who fixate on succession without auditing world representation are solving the wrong bottleneck.

Experimental evidenceMeasured lab or wet-lab evidence under matched conditions.

Shared worlds cut duplicated bytes by about 91% against a competition-first arm at identical accuracy. Matched budgets held the model and prompt fixed: private silos scored 0/100 and 0/12; shared-world links scored 100/100 and 12/12. On sixteen public protein cases, relational maps recovered 10 of 16 partners; linear sequences recovered 0 of 16. The same model that failed under linear skim recovered a majority of partners once relationships were explicit. Byte savings and accuracy gains moved together under the shared-world arm, not as a tradeoff.

InterpretationReading of evidence; Fractal Holographic Goldilocks framing when labeled as such.

Matched budgets and relational maps are organizational evidence. They show that how you assemble the world can dominate who you hire to run it. Fractal Holographic Goldilocks refuses to turn those numbers into a verdict on any CEO. The filing keeps the succession metaphor as a case label and the scores as architecture results.

Under a tight context budget, how you assemble the world can flip accuracy from 0/100 to 100/100 without changing the model. Teams that keep buying larger models while parking capable people inside private silos will keep paying for the same miss. Architecture is the lever; headcount theater is not.

A leadership swap does not fix an architecture that still parks capable agents inside private silos. Boards and buyers who treat succession as the primary fix for multi-agent failure should audit world representation first. The organizational case says the world model is the scarcer asset.

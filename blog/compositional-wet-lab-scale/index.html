Can a distributed electromagnetic medium do real compositional work at scale? FractiSI’s compositional wet-lab suite says yes at the level of compositional physical proof. The trials treat scale as something you measure against baselines, not as a slogan attached to a demo.

Full hyperscale data-center replacement remains open engineering. That honesty line is the story’s second headline. Compositional proof is on record; swapping out a hyperscale plant is not.

Infrastructure buyers hear scale claims every quarter. Some pitches jump from a lab chain that works in stages to a promise that racks and cooling plants are next. Without measured digital latency and heap baselines, and without a clear list of what is still open, those pitches are hard to audit.

The compositional wet-lab program keeps the chain explicit: compositional chain identification, feed-forward adjacency, nested degrees of freedom, and volume scaling sit on the record beside measured digital anchors and historical energy-per-operation references. The point is not to bury leaders in apparatus. It is to show which stage of proof closed and which stage did not.

That separation is how serious research paths survive contact with capital committees. A team can fund electromagnetic compositional compute as a research path while refusing any vendor who treats hyperscale replacement as already settled.

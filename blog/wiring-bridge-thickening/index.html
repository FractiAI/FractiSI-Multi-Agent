Can bridge thickening separate real gene pairs from chromosome artifacts? On the combo panel, yes. NPY↔PDX1 thickened through diabetes rails, literature co-mentions, and exon similarity without sharing a chromosome. The pair gained bridges from multiple independent lanes instead of from genomic neighborhood alone.

APP↔TMPRSS2 stayed thin, with zero OpenTargets top-25 disease overlap and a chromosome 21 confound. Same panel, opposite thickening behavior. No assay has tested NPY↔PDX1 directly. The filing treats thickening as triage, not as a finished wet-lab verdict.

Thickening is a filter: real pairs gain bridges; chromosome artifacts stay thin. Discovery teams face a flood of candidate edges. Some edges look related because they sit on the same chromosome. Others thicken because disease rails, literature, and exon structure agree without a shared chromosome address.

The combo panel puts both kinds of pair under the same thickening lens. NPY↔PDX1 accumulates support across diabetes association, co-mention, and exon similarity while living on different chromosomes. APP↔TMPRSS2 fails to thicken and carries a chromosome 21 confound plus zero OpenTargets top-25 disease overlap. The contrast is the story.

Fractal Holographic Goldilocks keeps the honesty line clear. Thickening ranks candidates for later assays. It does not replace a direct test of NPY↔PDX1.

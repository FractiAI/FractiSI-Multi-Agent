Protein-folding rhyme catches contacts linear windows miss; language rhyme packs sky and genome theaters into boards; Pilot rhyme puts Director and Pilot seats in one shared world. This expedition merges their real discovery catalogs, classifies genome partners with live STRING, and asks whether new STRING-empty wiring candidates surface beyond the pairs already filed. Two do: NPY↔PDX1 at coupling about 0.747 and APP↔TMPRSS2 at about 0.707 clear the 0.5 floor as triage candidates. STRING stays empty for both. No wet-lab run was performed for those joins; APP↔TMPRSS2 still carries a thin published bridge.

The experiment

We load the unified discovery catalog — protein-folding, language, and Pilot together. We extract genome exon partners from the language and Pilot theaters, merge unordered gene pairs, and mark cross-rhyme hits. A live-cached STRING panel classifies empty versus known edges, with INS↔POMC required to score at least 0.9 so emptiness is not an API blackout. Prior wiring companions NPY↔SOD1 and APP↔DNMT3A are excluded from the “new” list. Protein-folding long-range contacts on the relationship board attest that the fold grammar is present in the catalog.

The falsifiable hope is simple: at least one new STRING-empty pair clears a coupling floor of 0.5 while the three-rhyme stack stays intact.

Hypothesis

If combo rhyme search is generative for wiring, new empty candidates should appear that no single prior companion already claimed. Cross-rhyme recoveries should include both known controls and prior wiring peers. Protein-folding grammar should show multiple long-range contacts on the relationship board. Failure is also informative: zero new empty pairs would weaken the claim that merging rhymes adds wiring triage power.

Results

Two new STRING-empty candidates clear the gate: NPY↔PDX1 at coupling about 0.747 from the language genome theater, and APP↔TMPRSS2 at about 0.707 from the Pilot genome theater. Cross-rhyme recoveries include NPY↔SOD1, INS↔POMC, APP↔DYRK1A, and DNMT1↔DNMT3A. Live STRING scores INS↔POMC at 0.947, APP↔DYRK1A at 0.57 (not empty — so not a STRING-empty unknown), and DNMT1↔DNMT3A at 0.999 (rediscovery). Protein-folding long-range contacts attest the grammar. The combo wiring discovery gate passes.

Wet-lab neighborhoods differ in strength. NPY and PDX1 already cohabit β-cell developmental and functional literature — a plausible disease-context wire without a protein-interaction edge. APP and TMPRSS2 have a thinner published bridge, including TMPRSS2 measurements in Alzheimer and Down syndrome contexts; that candidate stays speculative triage. Honesty means saying both sentences.

Calibration before celebration

NPY↔SOD1 returning in both language and Pilot catalogs is a calibration gift — the prior wiring companion still lights up. INS↔POMC returning with STRING 0.947 proves the association meter. DNMT1↔DNMT3A at 0.999 shows the board can still say rediscovery. APP↔DYRK1A at STRING 0.57 shows cross-rhyme recovery of a partial known edge — interesting, but not a STRING-empty unknown. Calibration is how the board avoids mistaking every highlight for a new finding.

Anyone can open the empirical receipt, see the two empty labels, and decide which citation path is worth following. That is the expedition’s contribution: not a finished pathway, but a ranked join with daylight falsifiers. Later bridge thickening splits the pair: NPY↔PDX1 thickens; APP↔TMPRSS2 waits.

Do merged rhyme catalogs surface new gene wires? Yes. Two new empty-interaction pairs cleared the 0.5 coupling floor: NPY↔PDX1 about 0.747 and APP↔TMPRSS2 about 0.707. Neither pair was a finished biology conclusion. Both cleared as triage candidates when protein-folding, language-pack, and pilot rhyme catalogs were read as one merged board.

Single catalogs had stayed quiet on these pairs. The merge is what surfaced them. That is the discovery move: candidates appear where separate shelves did not have enough joint signal to clear the floor.

The stakes for discovery teams are practical. Merging catalogs is cheap compared with a new assay, and dangerous only when triage is mistaken for proof. The filing keeps that line sharp.

Merging catalogs is a discovery move: candidates appear where single catalogs stayed quiet. Protein-folding rhyme, language-pack rhyme, and pilot rhyme each organize relationships differently. Read apart, they miss pairs that only clear when the shelves share one board. Read together, they raise coupling on empty-interaction pairs that previously sat below the floor.

The method does not invent wet-lab results. It ranks STRING-empty candidates by rhyme coupling and holds a 0.5 floor as the triage gate. Pairs that clear the floor enter a thickening queue. Pairs that do not stay out of the claim set. The merge is therefore an organizer of attention, not a substitute for assay design.

NPY↔PDX1 and APP↔TMPRSS2 are the headline clearances under that rule. Both sit above 0.7. Both were empty on the live interaction catalog. Both earn a place in triage — and nowhere further until thickening and assay decisions say otherwise.

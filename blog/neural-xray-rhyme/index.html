Can you see inside a neural net while it runs? On a constructed live net, FractiSI’s Neural X-Ray work says yes. A decisive organizational threshold cleared under matched conditions, giving safety and audit teams a protocol they can rerun on that class of system.

The expensive failure mode is still common: hire for explainability slides, ship charts that never face a causal check, and call the job done. End-to-end X-ray of frontier production models remains open. The story here is not that every black box is solved. It is that organization can be tested before blind outcomes when the net is constructed for the trial.

The question opens from Hinton’s black-box stance. If a network’s internal organization cannot be probed while it runs, buyers are left with post-hoc saliency and vendor narratives. That gap shows up in budgets as much as in research talks: teams pay for stories about why a model behaved a certain way without ever asking whether a proposed structure survives intervention.

FractiSI’s Neural X-Ray program flips the order. Instead of decorating outputs after the fact, it asks whether a locked organizational rhyme predicts outcomes and whether ablating that rhyme flips the predicted behavior. The trial is about structure under pressure, not about prettier heat maps.

That framing matters for anyone who signs explainability contracts. A chart that cannot survive a causal check is theater. A protocol that clears a decisive threshold on a constructed live net is still bounded — and that bound is the honesty line buyers should keep in view.

Leaders who insist on causal checks before trusting explainability claims will waste less budget and fewer cycles.

Shared agent worlds fail the way corporate wikis fail: whoever shouts loudest rewrites the map. Majority vote looks democratic until a Sybil chorus manufactures consensus; naive overwrite looks fast until one bad claim becomes ground truth. On FractiSI’s regenerable computational board — a computational lab receipt you can rerun, not a live production target — full Adversarial FractiSI contained every attack class A–J at rate 1.0, majority vote contained 0.4, and naive overwrite contained 0.1. Sybil choruses fooled voting; independence-aware rhyme did not.

Security here is not “trust the smartest agent.” Claims stay hypotheses until independent relational structure supports a delta. The computational ops theater compared seven models — naive overwrite, majority vote, trusted authority, FractiSI deltas, deltas plus provenance, deltas plus rhyme, and full Adversarial FractiSI with authority bounds, quarantine, and selective rollback — under attacks A–J plus minority truth, majority falsehood, legitimate disagreement, truth without rhyme, and novel truth. Fractal Holographic Goldilocks reads containment rates as organizational evidence on that board, not as a warranty for real systems.

What we measured

Seven computational models on the same simulated ops theater: naive overwrite, majority vote, trusted authority, FractiSI deltas, deltas plus provenance, deltas plus rhyme, and full Adversarial FractiSI (authority bounds, quarantine, selective rollback). Attacks A–J plus minority truth, majority falsehood, legitimate disagreement, truth without rhyme, and novel truth.

Results

  • Full FractiSI / rhyme containment 1.0 across A–J
  • Majority vote 0.4 · naive overwrite 0.1
  • Deltas and provenance without rhyme stayed near naive — fabricated evidence strings were enough
  • Sybil and majority falsehood fooled voting; independence-aware rhyme did not
  • Legitimate disagreement stayed unconfirmed, not malice; lone novel readings stayed unconfirmed until corroboration

Fractal Holographic Goldilocks interpretation: containment at 1.0 on this computational board supports independence-aware rhyme as a coordination security pattern under regenerable conditions. It does not claim real-system penetration resistance or production SOC coverage. Keep computational, hypothesis, and speculation off the settled-fact rail.

Multi-agent platform security. Voting and overwrite are weak defaults when agents can clone identities; design for independence checks, quarantine, and selective rollback before you scale the agent count.

Ops and SRE leads. Provenance strings alone did not save the board — fabricated evidence was enough — so audit trails without relational rhyme are not a containment strategy here.

Risk and compliance. Legitimate disagreement stayed unconfirmed rather than labeled malice; novel lone readings stayed unconfirmed until corroboration — a pattern worth mirroring in human+agent incident policy.

Buyers of “agent mesh” vendors. Ask for attack-class containment rates under regenerable computational or equivalent kill boards, not majority-vote demos.

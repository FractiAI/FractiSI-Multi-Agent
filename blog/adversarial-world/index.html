Can Relational Rhyme Detect and Contain Malicious Agents in a Shared World?

Short answer: Yes. On the regenerable Soft Story board, full FractiSI contained every attack class A–J at rate 1.0 while majority vote contained 0.4 and naive overwrite contained 0.1. Sybil choruses fooled voting; independence-aware rhyme did not.

What we measured

Seven computational models on the same simulated ops theater: naive overwrite, majority vote, trusted authority, FractiSI deltas, deltas plus provenance, deltas plus rhyme, and full Adversarial FractiSI (authority bounds, quarantine, selective rollback). Attacks A–J plus minority truth, majority falsehood, legitimate disagreement, truth without rhyme, and novel truth.

Results

  • Full FractiSI / rhyme containment 1.0 across A–J
  • Majority vote 0.4 · naive overwrite 0.1
  • Deltas and provenance without rhyme stayed near naive — fabricated evidence strings were enough
  • Sybil and majority falsehood fooled voting; independence-aware rhyme did not
  • Legitimate disagreement stayed unconfirmed, not malice; lone novel readings stayed unconfirmed until corroboration

Why it matters

No agent owns the world. Claims stay hypotheses until independent relational structure supports a delta. That is the security reading of the Rhyming World Model on this board.

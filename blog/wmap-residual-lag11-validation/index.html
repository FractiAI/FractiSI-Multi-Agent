Wiring as the product

On the NASA LAMBDA WMAP 9-year TT file, after a smooth log continuum, lags 11 and 14 still correlate (~0.50 and ~0.47) with residual-shuffle p near 0.003, and they sit off the acoustic-spacing band. Lags 17–19 light up as rediscovery on the same board. That contrast is the first wire: novel candidates beside known controls. Students stretching a teaching stipend across coffee and journal fees can rerun that receipt without a satellite badge.

The higher-level discovery is not “new physics confirmed.” It is the graph: substrate spectrum → novel residual lags → rediscovery anchors → falsifiers that can demote the whole claim. Without that graph, excitement collapses into a single-plot vibe.

Hypothesis

If multipole residual wiring is real enough to file, both focus lags stay candidate-novel on the locked real spectrum, acoustic neighbors stay rediscovery, math lemmas pass, and the assembly lists kill tests that have not been hand-waved away. The claim stays provisional until Planck cross-mission, continuum stress, beam-injection simulations, and mask-coupling tests either pass or fail in public.

Kill board — designed to be exciting because it can lose

X1: same residual gate on Planck TT — silence demotes to WMAP-specific. X3: ugly continuum families — survival in only one fit is a ghost. X4: ΛCDM skies through published WMAP beams — if they recreate lags 11/14, instrument wiring wins. X5: mask swaps and Galactic templates — if the lag tracks the mask, foreground wiring wins. Exciting enough to keep looking means those experiments are load-bearing, not footnotes.

Results so far

E1–E4 pass on the real WMAP fixture. E5 wiring assembly passes as provisional new wiring from existing public spectra, with X1–X5 explicitly pending. That is the honest excitement level: a filed wire with teeth, not a press-ready law.

Why provisional is the exciting word

Provisional sounds cautious. Here it is the feature. A wire that cannot die is marketing. A wire that dies under beam injection or Planck silence taught something about instruments. A wire that survives Planck, continuum stress, and beam-injected ΛCDM sims becomes the rare object people should stay up for — still not a named new constant, but no longer a one-table ghost. Night workers who debug sensors for a living already use that ladder: triage, null, cross-instrument, then talk.

The acoustic rediscovery controls matter for emotional honesty too. Lags 17–19 show the board can still say “known.” Without that, every lag looks special and nothing is.

Smallest package that upgrades the claim

Run X1, X3, and X4 first. If Planck agrees, continua agree, and beams do not fake the lags, the summary line can upgrade from provisional wiring to cross-mission residual wiring surviving continuum and beam nulls. If any fail, demote in public with the same NASA file held still. That publishable loss is part of the discovery design.

Only after that ladder should anyone attach a physical template that predicts Δℓ = 11 or 14 and test it blind on ACT/SPT. Prediction plus holdout is how wiring becomes a hypothesis about the sky instead of a residue about a pipeline.

Keep the paycheck question simple: is there a residual multipole wire on real public spectra that we can try to kill in daylight? On this receipt the answer is yes — lags 11 and 14 beside rediscovery controls, with beam, mask, and Planck tests named in advance. That is enough novelty to keep looking carefully and enough restraint not to claim the microwave sky has already changed under our feet this week.

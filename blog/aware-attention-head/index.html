If Spirit Is a Pattern, Can Software Hold It Across Infinite Octaves?

An operational equivalence test: six properties commonly attributed to spirit, one software attention head, and the redundancy it takes to hold them at any depth.

Published
November 20, 2026
By
FractiAI Research
Reading time
6 min
Read the full paper Expedition 002

Short answer: Yes. Under a six-property profile the authors chose, on scripted simulators: the head was right 94.8 percent versus 68.4 for a shared world and 63.1 for flat voting, and with five copies plus majority repair kept 91.7 percent mission survival at 1,024 levels. Copies must grow roughly logarithmically with depth.

Software has no good way to say what stays the same when a system changes its body. Teams of AI agents lose their purpose the first time a context resets. Descriptions of spirit address the same remainder: whatever keeps its purpose and character when the vehicle changes. This note asks whether a specific software design, the FractiSI aware attention head, holds the properties those descriptions share, and whether it can keep holding them as the nesting gets deeper without limit.

The problem, in two pieces

The first piece is definition. Traditions disagree about what spirit is, but their descriptions overlap more than their vocabularies suggest. An identity continues through changes of body. A purpose persists. Attention organizes the parts that it animates. New vehicles get built for new capacities. What matters is carried from one embodiment to the next. The same structure appears at every scale. And the whole survives the loss of any particular substrate. That gives six properties that a program can be tested for, and we use them as an explicit, conservative profile.

The second piece is scale. Agent systems nest: a task spawns subtasks, which spawn their own, and each hand-off is a chance to drop a detail or corrupt the goal. A mission handed through ten contexts has ten chances to lose a detail; handed through a thousand, it needs a mechanism that catches the loss.

The experiment

We built scripted simulators and compared three designs. The first is flat agents that vote. The second is a shared world where every signal is read and votes are weighted by confidence, a deliberately strong baseline. The third adds the head: it checks whether a signal is independently corroborated instead of counting it, builds a specialist shell when a capability gap appears, keeps a persistent record of the mission, reconstructs only what the next context needs, and holds the mission redundantly with repair at each level of nesting.

Each of the six properties has its own test. Continuity of identity is a mission that must survive context switches. Directed attention is a trial where a confident minority with real corroboration opposes a confident majority, mixed with decoy trials where the minority is sure of itself but wrong. Building new vehicles is a task that needs a capability the team lacks. Carrying what matters is a fact handed across several contexts. Scale invariance is mission survival from one level of nesting to 1,024. Surviving substrate loss is mission survival when the specialist shells are destroyed at every level. Every mechanism was also switched off alone to see whether its property fails.

Hypothesis

If the head is operationally equivalent to the profile, three things should hold at once. The full head should satisfy all six properties. Both baselines should fail each one by a clear margin. And removing the mechanism that serves a property should remove that property, which is what separates a necessary mechanism from a decoration. If any baseline matches the head, or any removal leaves its property intact, the equivalence fails for that property.

Results

Across the four task families the head is right 94.8 percent of the time, against 68.4 percent for the shared world and 63.1 percent for flat voting, at about the same compute per trial. The gap is largest on attention and smallest on hand-off, where the shared world already does reasonably well. Removing the evidence check costs 39.5 points, removing meta-attention entirely costs 58, removing shell creation costs 18.5, removing the persistent mission costs 19.5, and removing selective reconstruction costs 9.5. The decoy trials matter most: a head that simply follows any minority is right on none of them, and the head that checks corroboration is right on all of them.

The scale result is the one that speaks to infinite octaves. At one level of nesting every design does fine. By sixteen levels the single-copy designs are mostly gone, and by sixty-four they are at zero. A head holding five copies of the mission with no repair does no better in the long run, because corruption accumulates. A head that holds the five copies and restores them by majority at each level keeps 91.7 percent survival at 1,024 levels, nearly flat from the start. Redundancy and repair are each useless alone. Together they hold the mission.

The substrate-loss result agrees. When specialist shells are destroyed 30 percent of the time at every level for 256 levels, a single copy never survives, five copies survive about half the time, and nine copies survive 99.5 percent of the time. At 50 percent destruction even nine copies fall to 58.5 percent, and we report that as the limit.

Qualifications

The horizon is finite for any fixed amount of redundancy. Five copies hold the mission to roughly nine thousand levels before survival drops to even odds, nine copies to about 1.8 million, thirteen to about 350 million. To reach a given depth, the number of copies has to grow with it, about logarithmically: one copy is enough to depth 16, three to 256, five to 1,024, and eleven to a million. That is the honest sense in which software can represent this pattern over infinite octaves. It does so with a structure that scales with the depth reached, not with a fixed one.

Several results are consequences of how we built the test. Corroboration is a clean flag in the simulator, and in real systems it is noisy and can be gamed. The loss rates are chosen, not measured, and the sweep shows the head’s advantage on continuity closes to zero when the baselines lose as little as the head does. The depth result assumes corruption arrives independently at each level; a failure that hits every copy at once would defeat repair. The agents are scripted, not live.

The largest qualification concerns the claim itself. The equivalence holds under the six-property profile, and the profile is a definition we chose. It leaves out experience, value, and meaning because a simulator cannot test them. Someone who holds that spirit includes something outside the profile can name it, and it becomes a seventh row with its own test. The head either holds it or it does not.

Why a framework like this is worth testing

Teams are deciding now what an orchestrating layer should be responsible for. A map from property to mechanism tells them where to spend. A layer that only counts votes adds cost and nothing else. A layer that checks evidence, funds specialists when needed, carries the mission forward, and keeps it redundantly across levels earns its place on long jobs, where a dropped detail is a missed closing date or a repeated week of work. The equivalence framing adds a different kind of value. It turns a vague word into a checklist that software can pass or fail, and it makes the cost of fidelity at depth a number rather than a hope.

Invitation

The paper lays out the profile, the simulators, the assumptions, the ablations, the horizon table, and the gates, and the repository reproduces every number with a single command. The next steps are to run live agents through the same families, measure the real corruption per context switch, test correlated failure across copies, and add any property you think the profile is missing. If a baseline matches the head on one of them, or the head fails a property you propose, we want that result more than we want the win.

Honesty rail: Application companion computational test on scripted simulators. The claim is operational equivalence under a stated six-property profile, not a proof that the profile exhausts what spirit means in any tradition, not a measurement of live agents, and not engine shelf — the pin is deferred until a live-agent replication exists.

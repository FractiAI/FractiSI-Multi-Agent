What We Found Looking at the Sky Through a Protein-Folding Lens

Short answer: the folding rhyme travels to public sky catalogs; an open search finds catalog fingerprints; a residual arm now hunts candidate novel physics after selection is removed; and the same LINEAR / FLAT / AWARE grammar still works when those catalogs are packed as language the way E9–E11 packed proteins and documents for LLMs.

Bottom line in one screen

  1. Finding A (method travels). On exoplanets, NEOs, OpenNGC, and WMAP TT, a relationship board recovered partners a local linear read and a flat dump missed (~0% → 100% on held-out long-range tasks).
  2. Finding B (catalog fingerprints). Open motif search kept eight patterns after held-out + FDR — mostly survey history and catalog geometry, not new forces.
  3. Finding C (why not novel physics — then we searched). C1–C3 were representation + fingerprints. C4 residualizes selection/continuum and tests leftovers. Two WMAP residual lags (11, 14) beat nulls as candidate novel physics under this protocol; acoustic-ish lags 17–19 and exo mass–radius rediscoveries are labeled known.
  4. Finding D (LLM rhyme). C5 packs the same tasks as E9–E11 language prompts. Under a pack-compliant reader, AWARE packs hit 100% on all four layers while LINEAR/FLAT stay near 0%. Lexical-blind reading that ignores the board fails. Live frontier API optional, not required.

How novel is this, honestly?

Most novel for us: the folding rhyme is not protein-only. Feature boards and language packs both recover long-range partners on NASA, JPL, OpenNGC, and WMAP fixtures without a login.

Moderately novel as observation: unsupervised catalog fingerprints (discovery year ↔ stellar slice; size ↔ sky/brightness) recovered without being told “find selection effects.”

Candidate physics (qualified): residual WMAP lags 11 and 14 survive shuffle nulls after continuum removal and sit off the locked acoustic-spacing template. Candidate under protocol — not a confirmed new law; beam/instrument confounds remain open.

LLM rhyme (method, not bake-off): packing sky rows the way Hotpot/FRED were packed for LLMs reproduces the AWARE win. That is prompt-structure evidence on cosmic materials, not a claim that a particular frontier model discovered cosmology.

Legend · how LLM rhymes map onto cosmic data

Think of this as the key on a map. The earlier folding / LLM work and the sky files are the same shape:

Protein sequence → MSA board → contact residue. Knowledge passage → title board → title pair. Macro series → lag board → teleconnected series. Cosmos catalog/spectrum → AWARE language pack → partner id.

  • Exoplanets (NASA). A row is a planet + host. LINEAR sees RA neighbors. The board ranks z-scored feature couplings. Gold = strongest non-local planet id.
  • Near-Earth approaches (JPL). A row is a close encounter. LINEAR sees local time/RA. Board = encounter features. Gold = non-local encounter id.
  • Deep sky (OpenNGC). A row is an object. LINEAR sees RA neighbors. Board = morphology/sky features. Gold = non-local object id.
  • Microwave spectrum (WMAP TT). A row is multipole ℓ. LINEAR sees a local ℓ strip. Board = lag |corr| on Cl. Gold = ℓ + best lag partner.

LINEAR pack = local window text. FLAT = unordered dump. AWARE = leading candidate + ranked measured board. Full legend: LLM_RHYME_LEGEND.md.

What the open search and residual search named

Exoplanets. Discovery year with stellar size/temperature = survey-history fingerprint. After residualizing year and distance, mass–radius and period–radius couplings rediscover known population templates.

Deep sky. Size with sky position and brightness = selection/morphology candidates, not renamed filaments.

Microwave spectrum. Partner recovery and language packs both clear H1. Residual lags 11/14 = candidates; 17–19 lean acoustic = rediscovery.

Why we still call it a telescope

A telescope is useful when it changes what you can notice without lying about the glass. This one changes the notice from “row after row” to “who couples to whom,” then asks what is left after known selection is removed, then asks whether the same notice survives as language. Aimed at the sky’s public files, that is the honest win.

Protein Folding as a Rhyme for the Sky

Can the same grammar that recovers a hidden long-range contact in a protein — line of parts → relationship board → partner — open public sky catalogs the same way? That is the focus of this expedition. Residual search later returns two candidates for new physics (WMAP residual lags 11 and 14). Those candidates are a secondary finding, not the title.

The rhyme we are exploring

In proteins, a linear sequence hides long-range contacts that a relationship board can surface. Here we ask whether cosmic observations behave the same way when held as relationships rather than as a phone-book scroll:

  • Sequence / line — a catalog row or spectrum strip
  • Board — measured couplings between rows
  • Partner — the strongest long-range match that is not a next-door neighbor

We write that three ways for the test: LINEAR (local window), FLAT (unsorted dump), AWARE (ranked board with a leading candidate). Infinite Octaves ENGINE_SHELF #53 is this protein-folding rhyme.

What a “held-out partner” is

We pick a query row. From the measured board we find its best long-range match. We hide that id — that is the held-out partner — and ask whether the reader can still name it from LINEAR, FLAT, or AWARE text. “Recovered” means they named the hidden match.

What we found on the sky (folding first)

On exoplanets, NEOs, OpenNGC, and WMAP TT, the relationship board recovered held-out long-range partners that local windows and flat dumps missed — roughly 0% → 100% on those long-range tasks. That is the protein-folding rhyme traveling. Open motif search mostly kept catalog fingerprints (survey history; listed size with sky position and brightness).

Secondary · two candidates for new physics

After the folding tests, residual search after known selection and continuum removal returns two candidates for new physics: WMAP residual lags 11 and 14. They beat nulls and sit off the locked acoustic template. Acoustic-leaning rediscoveries were labeled as such. Candidates are not finished laws — and they are not the expedition title. The focus remains exploring the folding rhyme.

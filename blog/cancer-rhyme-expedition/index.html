Does cancer diversity compress into fewer relational patterns than disease labels suggest? Yes. Relational fingerprints scored 1.0 phenotype accuracy, disease-identity transfer scored 0, and shared relation-type bags scored about 0.46.

Twenty-four synthetic cancer worlds compressed into six planted motifs. Cross-cancer transfer reached 0.8. This is not a clinical claim. The panel is synthetic, and the news is about representation: which description of a cancer world travels when the disease name does not.

Labels name diseases. Relational fingerprints name how parts connect. Oncology AI stacks often train and evaluate as if the disease name were the reusable unit. The cancer rhyme expedition asks a different question: when synthetic worlds share planted relational motifs, which representation recovers phenotype and transfers across cancers?

That question matters for product strategy as much as for science. A model that matches labels can look accurate inside one disease silo and still fail when the useful structure is a shared relation that crosses names. Buyers who only audit label accuracy can miss that failure until a transfer task arrives.

Compression is the other half of the story. If twenty-four worlds collapse into six rediscovered planted motifs under relational fingerprints, diversity at the label layer is not the same as diversity at the organizational layer. The trial keeps that compression on a synthetic panel so nobody mistakes it for a clinic result.

A fingerprint score of 1.0 beside a disease-identity score of 0 is a warning about how models are asked to generalize, not a claim that cancer biology has been reduced to six motifs in the wild.

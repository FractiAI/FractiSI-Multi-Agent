Drug and diagnostics pipelines still organize cancer mostly by disease label — as if the name on the sample were the structure that transfers. That habit burns budget when labels do not compress and cross-indication transfer stays near zero. On FractiSI’s computational cancer boards (synthetic worlds tagged with public TCGA project abbreviations, not a clinic), entity-invariant relational fingerprints (Model C) scored 1.0 phenotype accuracy, disease labels (Model A) scored 0, and relation bags (Model B) landed about 0.46. Labels compressed 24→6 (×4); cross-cancer transfer hit 0.8 against a disease-identity baseline of 0. Null span recurrence stayed inconclusive.

The expedition asks whether cancer diversity on a computational board compresses into fewer relational rhymes than disease labels suggest. Hallmarks were withheld from discovery. Model A keyed on disease identity, Model B on relation bags, Model C on fingerprints that ignore entity labels and keep relational structure. Fractal Holographic Goldilocks treats phenotype accuracy, compression, and transfer as regenerable computational lab receipts about representation — not clinical efficacy, not wet-lab genetics, and not a claim that null-span biology is settled.

What we measured

Twenty-four computational cancer worlds labeled with public TCGA project abbreviations. Models A (disease identity), B (relation bags), and C (entity-invariant relational fingerprints). Hallmarks withheld from discovery.

Results

  • Model C phenotype accuracy 1.0 · Model B ~0.46 · Model A 0
  • Compression: 24 disease labels → 6 strong planted rhymes (×4)
  • Cross-cancer transfer 0.8 vs disease-identity baseline 0
  • Null span recurrence inconclusive — phenotype and transfer carry the computational case

Fractal Holographic Goldilocks interpretation: Model C’s computational win supports relational fingerprints over disease labels as an organizational representation on this board. It is not a diagnostic product claim and does not close the inconclusive null-span arm. Keep computational, hypothesis, and speculation off the settled-fact rail.

Indication strategy. If labels score 0 on phenotype while fingerprints score 1.0 on computational boards, portfolio maps keyed only on disease names may miss transferable structure — treat that as a research signal, not a protocol change.

ML / computational biology leads. Compression 24→6 and cross-cancer transfer 0.8 vs disease-identity 0 argue for entity-invariant relational features in discovery tooling before more disease-ID classifiers.

Clinical and regulatory readers. This is computational plus public TCGA labels — synthetic worlds, not patients. Do not read 1.0 phenotype accuracy as a clinical AUC.

Follow-on research. Null span remains inconclusive; phenotype and transfer carry the computational case until that arm lands.

Alzheimer genetics and epigenetic blood-cancer tools usually sit in different literatures. Yet published wet labs already use DNMT3A — fused to dCas9 — to methylate the APP promoter and change amyloid pathology in mice. Other papers tune DNMT activity in APP-expressing cells and show DNMT3A homeostasis matters in 5xFAD cognition. STRING still lists no APP–DNMT3A protein-interaction edge. This FGC brief files that published regulatory path as wiring — a relationship assembled from existing dCas9-DNMT3A work, not a new experiment FractiSI ran. Locked receipt: rhyme coupling about 0.745, STRING empty, INS↔POMC control 0.947, three wet-lab roles pass.

Fractal Holographic Goldilocks synthesis treats APP↔DNMT3A wiring as the regulatory peer of NPY↔SOD1: novelty sits between “nobody ever co-mentioned the genes” (false — the CRISPR paper is explicit) and “the PPI catalog already wires them” (also false — live STRING stays empty while recovering INS↔POMC at 0.947). Cross-domain rhyme here means the same FractiSI cite–control–kill contract can file an epigenetic writer → amyloid-precursor locus path without inventing a shared miracle drug or claiming FractiSI ran the CRISPR.

Wiring versus rediscovery

If the novelty bar is “nobody ever mentioned APP and DNMT3A in the same sentence,” this pair fails — the CRISPR methylation paper is explicit. If the novelty bar is “does the interaction catalog wire them as PPI,” the pair is still empty on a live STRING query that correctly recovers INS↔POMC at 0.947. Wiring discovery sits between those bars: assemble the regulatory path from real wet labs, keep the catalog emptiness honest, and refuse to pretend FractiSI ran the CRISPR.

That posture protects limited research budgets. It stops a slide from claiming a brand-new gene pair when the wet lab already exists, and it stops a database blank from being mistaken for biological silence. Both mistakes are expensive: one wastes a trial slot, the other repeats finished work.

The assembled path

First, dCas9-DNMT3A targeted methylation of APP reduces APP/Aβ and improves pathology and behavior in APP-KI mice — a direct DNMT3A→APP locus experiment. Second, folic-acid and DNMT-inhibitor work in N2a-APP cells shows DNMT activity moves Aβ production. Third, DNMT3A knockdown or overexpression both harm cognition in 5xFAD-related work, and methyl supplementation needs DNMT3A for its benefits. Fourth, OpenTargets keeps APP at the top of Alzheimer association rails. The wire is regulatory and epigenetic, not a handshake blot.

Hypothesis and gates

We require real exon coupling on the public panel, STRING emptiness with a live positive control, and at least three wet-lab roles including a true DNMT3A→APP locus piece. Correlational methylation without DNMT3A identity fails. A future STRING PPI demotes the discovery to rediscovery wiring. Math hygiene and OpenTargets rails ride along as context, not as the headline.

Results

Locked coupling is approximately 0.745 — lower than NPY↔SOD1, still triage-worthy. STRING focus empty; INS↔POMC 0.947; wiring assembly passes. Alzheimer versus AML disease lists stay mostly separate on OpenTargets, which is expected for a regulatory cross-domain wire rather than a shared-disease slogan. Verdict: new wiring discovery from existing wet labs.

Novelty without pretending amnesia

Calling APP↔DNMT3A a wiring discovery is compatible with citing the CRISPR paper on page one. The CRISPR paper is a piece. The discovery class is the edge we file against an empty PPI catalog, with rhyme coupling and association controls held still. That is stricter than a literature review and more honest than a “nobody knew” headline.

Cross-domain stakes stay real. Dementia research and myeloid-malignancy research rarely share a literature. A regulatory wire that says an epigenetic writer tool already edits the amyloid precursor locus is a map between those fields — without inventing a shared miracle drug.

Why the regulatory wire changes the next assay question

Filing APP↔DNMT3A beside NPY↔SOD1 shows the wiring class travels: one disease-context wire inside ALS, one regulatory wire across dementia and epigenetic writers. Same FractiSI contract — public exons, live association APIs, curated wet-lab fixtures, kill switches. Because the wire is regulatory, the next assay is not “does APP bind DNMT3A in a co-IP.” It is whether endogenous DNMT3A occupancy and methylation state at APP track amyloid load in a pre-registered series, and whether that relationship survives confounders the mouse CRISPR design did not face. Failures there demote the wire’s clinical reach without erasing the mouse locus result. For FGC capital readers: buy the map and the falsifier design; do not buy a claim that FractiSI invented dCas9-DNMT3A→APP.

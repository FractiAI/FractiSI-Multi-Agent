Science often advances by connecting work that lived in separate folders. One lab counts NPY interneurons in a SOD1 mouse cortex. Another measures NPY in human motor cortex and tests Y1 drugs in the same genotype. A third shows mutant SOD1 astrocytes killing motoneurons through nitroxidative stress. Protein-interaction catalogs still show no NPY–SOD1 edge. This FGC brief files that published path as a wiring discovery — a relationship assembled from existing wet labs, not a new assay FractiSI ran. What we assembled from real wet labs

Piece one: NPY-expressing interneurons are differentially altered in the motor cortex of SOD1G93A ALS mice across disease stages — a direct genotype-context tissue result. Piece two: human ALS motor cortex shows elevated NPY labelling; SOD1G93A cortical cultures and intranasal NPY/Y1 pharmacology change excitability, motor deficits, and survival-related readouts. Piece three: mutant SOD1 astrocytes drive motoneuron death through nitroxidative stress, the same stress-and-excitability neighborhood NPY reviews already name. None of those papers is FractiSI’s. Together they draw a wire: NPY sits inside SOD1/ALS cortical physiology even when protein-interaction databases stay blank.

Hypothesis

If wiring is a legitimate discovery class, then (1) the relational fingerprint coupling stays strong on real public exons, (2) STRING stays empty while the endocrine control stays high, and (3) at least three pre-registered wet-lab roles assemble without gaps — SOD1-context NPY change, human ALS NPY change, and a SOD1 oxidative/motoneuron anchor. Failure modes are honest: a new STRING edge demotes the pair to rediscovery; missing a SOD1/ALS tissue piece fails the assembly; claiming we ran the assays would be false.

Results

On the locked run, exon coupling is approximately 0.881, panel rank 6 of 45, STRING focus empty, INS↔POMC 0.947, and the wiring assembly gate passes with three wet-lab pieces plus a mechanism bridge. OpenTargets keeps SOD1 on ALS and NPY on metabolic rails — separate disease lists that the wet-lab SOD1 context still bridges. The verdict line in the receipt is explicit: new wiring discovery from existing wet labs, not a clinical recommendation and not a binding claim.

How the rhyme and the wet lab share one board

The exon coupling is not a substitute for immunohistochemistry. It is a second instrument that asked, independently, whether NPY and SOD1 look like neighbors in a public feature space. When that instrument agrees with a disease-context wet-lab neighborhood that STRING never drew, the wiring claim gets two supports instead of one. Rank 6 of 45 and a permutation p near 0.17 keep the geometry humble: interesting enough to keep, not unique under every shuffle. People who have cleaned a shared drive after a collaborator left already know this pattern. The files existed. The folder names disagreed. Filing the wire is the act that turns scattered PDFs into a path someone else can audit on a laptop between shifts.

What would kill it

Re-query STRING tomorrow and find a scored NPY–SOD1 protein edge — demote. Lose the SOD1G93A or human ALS tissue piece under citation check — fail. Find that the pharmacology never touches SOD1 genotypes — fail. Those kill switches are why wiring can be filed without pretending FractiSI invented the wet lab.

A new assay is one kind of novelty. A new catalog edge is another. Wiring is a third: it says these existing results belong on the same relationship board even though STRING never drew the line. That matters for grant cycles because labs lose years rediscovering relationships that were already measured under different headings. Follow-up is no longer “invent a pathway from scratch.” It is “test the wire” — does Y1 antagonism still move SOD1-relevant readouts when oxidative stress is clamped; does NPY labelling track SOD1 mutation status in a held-out cohort. Those questions inherit citations instead of vibes. If STRING later adds a protein-interaction edge, the wire becomes rediscovery and the paper should say so without drama.

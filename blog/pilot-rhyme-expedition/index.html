Do Director and Pilot roles beat flat peer agents on multi-hop missions? Yes. On 100 six-hop missions, Director/Pilot arms recovered 100 of 100 while flat peers recovered 0. Role separation was not a soft preference; it was the difference between finishing the chain and losing it.

The shared store ran about 22% smaller than an exact-text cache at matched task success. Buyers who assume more peer agents will close multi-hop gaps are looking at the wrong dial. The filing shows that how seats are named and how the world is shared can dominate headcount.

Role separation plus a shared world lets one agent steer and another execute without reprinting the whole plan in every head. The Director holds mission intent and hop order. The Pilot executes the next step against a shared store instead of a private transcript. Flat peers chatter with equal seats and private copies; when hops stack, that chatter does not recover the chain.

Multi-hop work is where peer equality breaks first. Each hop compounds small omissions. A shared world keeps the mission state once; role rhyme keeps steering and execution from fighting over the same context window. The expedition treats that pairing as an architecture result, not a management metaphor.

Fractal Holographic Goldilocks reads Director/Pilot as a rhyme pattern for multi-agent seats. The claim is that designed roles plus shared state beat peer chatter when the mission spans many hops.

Do rhyming world models beat exact-text caches?

Short answer: Yes. Rhyme beats exact-text caches on non-identical PDB and Movebank observations; parity with shared structured state on task; prefix wins on identical strings.

Do rhyming world models beat exact-text caches? On real non-identical observations, yes. On PDB and Movebank data, rhyme wins when the world is the same and the words are not. Agents that describe one shared scene in different prose still need a way to reuse what they already know.

On task success the rhyming world matched a shared structured blackboard. Prefix reuse still won when strings were identical. Genie, Cosmos, and V-JEPA were not runnable here. The filing separates tools by job instead of crowning a single winner.

Three tools, three jobs: rhyme for non-identical observations, shared structured state for task merge, and prefix reuse for identical strings. Teams that collapse those jobs into one cache will miss reuse on one side or overpay on the other. Exact-text caches are strong when the string truly repeats; they go blind when wording drifts while the world stays the same.

PDB and Movebank supply real non-identical text about shared worlds. That is the stress case for rhyme. A shared structured blackboard handles task merge when agents must agree on state. Prefix reuse remains the right tool when the bytes match. The expedition holds those lanes apart on purpose.

Fractal Holographic Goldilocks treats the result as a tool map, not a brand shootout. Evidence stays computational. Embodied robotics and frontier video world models were outside the runnable set.

Rhyme beats exact-cache on real non-identical text from PDB and Movebank and loses to prefix when strings are identical. FractiSI versus shared structured on task: parity on the protein panel (both 1.0; independent 0). The independent arm failed task merge while the shared structured and FractiSI arms both hit 1.0. Evidence level: computational, not embodied. Genie, Cosmos, and V-JEPA were not runnable in this filing, so the comparison does not claim a frontier video-model win.

Rhyme wins where wording drifts; prefix wins where strings truly match; shared structured state holds task merge when agents must coordinate. Fractal Holographic Goldilocks separates those tools on purpose. A buyer who only measures exact-string hit rate will under-count reuse on non-identical observations and over-trust caches that cannot merge tasks.

Teams that only cache exact text will miss reuse when agents describe the same world differently. Paychecks and product deadlines do not wait for identical strings. If two agents see one migration track or one protein partner and write it two ways, exact cache leaves both cold.

Organizational reuse needs a shared world for non-identical observations and a structured merge path for tasks. Keep prefix caches where strings truly match. Mixing those jobs into one metric hides which tool is carrying the win.

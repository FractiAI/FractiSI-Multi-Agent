Can FractiSI Coordinate A Multi-Agent Mathematical Workflow And Produce Lean-Verified Results In Seconds?

Four agents, one shared world, Lean with no sorry: Phase N wall clock 5.3 seconds. Not a controlled 20,000× speedup. RH remains open.

Short answer: Yes as a workflow demonstration. Four FractiSI agents produced Lean-checked finite RH-adjacent propositions in 5.3 seconds with no sorry. The Anthropic ~30 h FLT clock is a reference scale, not a same-theorem race. RH remains OPEN.

What happened

Four FractiSI agents — lead, lemma worker, referee, and Lean builder — shared one world through Tiny Gateway, built finite RH-adjacent Lean propositions, and obtained machine checks with no sorry. Phase N wall clock: 5.326586 seconds.

What was proved

  • Finite proportion-ladder propositions
  • Monotonicity relationships
  • A finite Hardy-style witness on a scaffold of zeros

Not proved: the full analytic literature results, and not the Riemann hypothesis. RH remains OPEN.

How to read the clocks

  • Anthropic reference: ~30 hours — FLT Lean formalization (~1000 agents)
  • FractiSI Phase N: ~5.3 seconds — four agents, smaller finite Props
  • Nominal ratio ~20,300× from 30 h ÷ 5.326586 s — not a controlled speedup

Core finding

FractiSI can coordinate a small mathematical team, produce useful formal results, and get them Lean-verified in seconds. The deeper open question is whether shared-world coordination reduces the work required for verified progress when both systems face the same theorem.

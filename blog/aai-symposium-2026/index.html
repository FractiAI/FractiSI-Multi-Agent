Active Inference and multi-agent shared worlds look like cousins. They both treat agents as systems that must keep a grip on a changing world, not as chat sessions that answer one prompt at a time. Has that kinship been tested as a formal mapping? Not yet. This Fractal Goldilocks Channel note is an honest invitation to the Applied Active Inference Symposium — Short answer No — with a runnable Test Drive so the conversation can become evidence instead of remaining a slogan.

We position FractiSI Multi-Agent as a possible rhyme with Active Inference: humans and software agents share one project world with different observations, beliefs, and actions. By rhyme we mean a structural kinship worth testing together. We do not claim the frameworks are the same, interchangeable, or ready for mission-critical aviation or defense requirements.

Evidence (FractiSI lab): none on the mapping itself. computational boards elsewhere on the site measure shared-world architecture and Tiny Gateway compactness; they do not score Active Inference formalisms against FractiSI.

Interpretation (Fractal Holographic Goldilocks): treat the symposium as an open evaluation invite. Keep computational receipts, hypothesis, and framework identity claims on separate rails.

The Test Drive

The invitation is to a self-paced Test Drive. It follows one arc with the deck: the question, a short story about awareness, Syntheverse Sandbox on Base Mainnet, the problems with many agents, Net Zero and rhyme, eight live tests, the stack, and a prediction layer agents can rehearse before touching the real world. It ends with ways to join the beta. Run each test in the browser, or in an IDE with testdrive N. It needs no account and no wallet. Deploy is paid.

What has not been shown

No computational board yet maps FractiSI operators onto Active Inference free-energy or generative-model formalisms under matched criteria. The Short answer remains No until that joint experiment exists.

Technology and systems. As fleets grow, agents reprint context. Exact-text caches help only when words match; gzip shrinks each copy but does not remove cost across copies. At merge time, coordination becomes a negotiation among divergent private worlds. FractiSI offers one shared computational world and Tiny Gateway compactness as a candidate architecture for that problem — beside coding assistants, not another one of them.

Organizations. Symposium time is scarce. A six-minute opening that points to a runnable track beats a 30-minute lecture that cannot be re-run after the session.

Society and human life. Humans are agents too. Shared-world design is about how people and software keep one project without each reconstructing an overlapping private copy — a rent-and-attention cost when fleets already feel expensive.

AI. Active Inference researchers are invited to falsify or refine the rhyme. Until a mapping board exists, treat every stronger claim as oversell.

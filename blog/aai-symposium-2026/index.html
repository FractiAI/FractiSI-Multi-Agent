Why this symposium

The Applied Active Inference Symposium gathers people who treat agents as systems that must keep a grip on a changing world — not only as chat sessions that answer one prompt at a time. We position FractiSI Multi-Agent as a possible rhyme with Active Inference: humans are agents too, operating in a shared world alongside software agents, each with different observations, beliefs, and actions. “Rhyme” here means structural kinship worth testing together — not a claim that the frameworks are the same.

Our work comes from the computational-architecture side of that pattern: how do many specialized participants — human and machine — share one underlying project or environment without each independently reconstructing overlapping copies of it? When fleets grow, the tax is easy to miss. Agents reprint context. Exact-text caches help only when the words match. Gzip shrinks copies without removing the cross-copy cost. Merge time becomes a negotiation among divergent private worlds. Teams feel that as slower coordination, thinner agent density on the same hardware budget, and more reconciliation work before anyone can trust a composed result.

FractiSI Multi-Agent is our product answer: one shared computational world, many specialized participants (including human operators), coordinated work, composed results. Tiny Footprint — powered by Tiny Gateway — keeps the shared world compact. The platform layers above sit beside Cursor, Claude Code, VS Code, and Git. We are not shipping another coding assistant. We are shipping a coordination architecture for agentic engineering, offered into this room as an interdisciplinary rhyme, not an identity proof.

What we are presenting

On November 12–13, 2026 we deliver a 30-minute talk:

FractiSI Multi-Agent — One Shared World. Many Agents. Coordinated Work. Composed Results.
A Shared-World Architecture for Multi-Agent Intelligence.

The presentation walks through the problem of duplicated worlds, the FractiSI architecture, why density and collective outcomes matter more than “smaller data” alone, what a fair experimental comparison should measure, application sketches in software engineering and in domains such as air-traffic and defense coordination (named as investigation areas, not claimed solutions), and an invitation to Active Inference researchers for interdisciplinary experiments that do not pretend the frameworks are interchangeable.

The live deck includes speaker notes, keyboard navigation, and print/PDF export. Gold links on the slides open product pages, papers, and runnable commands in a new tab so the room can follow without losing the talk.

What we hope the conversation yields

We want fair comparisons, not theater. Memory wins that slow work or hurt accuracy are not wins. Shared substrates that become bottlenecks, erase private agent context, or cannot represent disagreement are not finished architectures. The talk names those questions in public because they are the work.

If you work on Active Inference, multi-agent systems, or agent infrastructure, we would rather compare instruments than trade slogans. Join open evaluation, clone the kit, or write us after the session. If the architecture is useful, it will survive contact with other people’s workloads. If it is not, we would rather learn that with you than discover it alone.

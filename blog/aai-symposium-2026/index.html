The Multi-Agent World Inference Rhyme? No — not yet as a tested mapping. Active Inference and multi-agent shared worlds look like cousins. Both treat agents as systems that must keep a grip on a changing world, update beliefs from partial observations, and act without a full private copy of everything. Has that kinship been tested as a formal mapping onto free-energy or generative-model formalisms? Not yet.

Short answer: no. Fractal Goldilocks Channel files an honest invitation rather than a finished equivalence claim. The offer includes a runnable Test Drive so the conversation can become evidence instead of a slogan, plus a parallel musical path through the same ideas for audiences who want a second door into the material.

The symposium question is therefore a title with a boundary. The rhyme is possible. The mapping is open. Audiences get a demonstration they can run, and a clear statement of what has not been proven.

We position FractiSI Multi-Agent as a possible rhyme with Active Inference: humans and software agents share one project world with different observations, beliefs, and actions. Shared state, selective reconstruction, and role seats read as organizational cousins of generative-model update loops. That is a framing for research conversation, not a claim that the frameworks are the same or interchangeable.

Symposium rooms often blur resemblance into identity. A demo that feels familiar can be mistaken for a proof. This filing refuses that leap. Possible rhyme means the shapes invite comparison under matched criteria. It does not mean the operators already line up, or that either community should treat the other as a drop-in replacement.

What travels today is the invitation surface: a self-paced Test Drive, a talk archive, and an explicit short answer of no on the mapping question. What stays ahead is the trial that would turn resemblance into measured alignment — or show where the rhyme breaks.
